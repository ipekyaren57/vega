About this prototype. VEGA is a NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 concept for the astronaut health self-assessment challenge. All health values are simulated; thresholds are illustrative and this is not a medical device.
NASA resources. Indicator choice and the “hazard” tags on every body region follow NASA's Human Research Program and its five hazards of human spaceflight (space radiation, isolation & confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, hostile closed environments): nasa.gov/hrp/hazards. Communication delays per environment are based on HRP's “distance from Earth” hazard (Mars: up to ~20 min one way; compressed in this demo).
Evidence used in the thresholds and explanations. Bone loss 1–1.5 %/month on 4–6-month missions: NASA HRP – Risk of Spaceflight-Induced Bone Changes. Astronaut sleep (just over 6 h/night on ISS vs 8.5 h scheduled): Barger et al., Lancet Neurology 2014, NASA/NSBRI-funded (summary). Vision changes on long missions: NASA HRP. Future work: calibrate personal baselines with astronaut physiological data from the NASA Open Science Data Repository.
SI disclosure. The code of this prototype was written with the help of an AI model (Claude, Anthropic). The concept and interaction design — visor HUD, arm panel, voice assistant VEGA, adaptive low-stress interface and the link to Earth — are by the team. Voice uses the browser's built-in Web Speech API (works best in Chrome).
